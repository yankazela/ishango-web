## Overview

For British expats and international professionals, the choice between staying in the UK and relocating to Portugal has become one of the most discussed financial decisions in recent years. Portugal's former **Non-Habitual Resident (NHR)** regime offered sweeping tax advantages that attracted tens of thousands of expats — but it was reformed in 2024.

This guide compares the **UK tax system** with **Portugal's new IFICI regime** (which replaced NHR) to help you decide which is right for your situation.

---

## The UK Tax System (2026)

### Income Tax

The UK uses a progressive income tax system with a tax-free personal allowance.

| Income Range | Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to £12,570 | 0% (Personal Allowance) |
| £12,571 – £50,270 | 20% (Basic Rate) |
| £50,271 – £125,140 | 40% (Higher Rate) |
| Above £125,140 | 45% (Additional Rate) |

> Note: The personal allowance is tapered above £100,000 — it reduces by £1 for every £2 earned above this threshold.

### UK Capital Gains Tax (2026)

| Asset Type | Basic Rate | Higher Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Residential property | 18% | 24% |
| Other assets | 10% | 20% |

### UK Dividend Tax

| Rate Band | Dividend Tax Rate |
|---|---|
| Basic | 8.75% |
| Higher | 33.75% |
| Additional | 39.35% |

### UK Pension Taxation

- State pension is taxable income
- Private pensions: 25% lump sum tax-free, remainder taxed as income
- Lifetime Allowance abolished (2024)

## Portugal's IFICI Regime (2026)

The **IFICI (Incentivo Fiscal à Capitalização das Empresas e Investigação, Ciência e Inovação)** regime replaced the NHR in January 2024.

### Who Qualifies for IFICI?

To qualify, you must:

1. **Not have been a Portuguese tax resident** in the preceding 5 years
2. **Work in a qualifying activity** — primarily tech, R&D, scientific, highly qualified professional roles

### IFICI Income Tax Rate

Qualifying income is taxed at a **flat 20%** for a period of **10 years**.

| Income Type | IFICI Rate |
|---|---|
| Employment income (qualifying activity) | 20% |
| Self-employment income (qualifying activity) | 20% |
| Non-qualifying Portuguese income | Standard rates (up to 48%) |

### Standard Portuguese Tax Brackets (2026)

| Income Range | Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to €7,703 | 13.25% |
| €7,703 – €11,623 | 18% |
| €11,623 – €16,472 | 23% |
| €16,472 – €21,321 | 26% |
| €21,321 – €27,146 | 32.75% |
| €27,146 – €39,791 | 37% |
| €39,791 – €51,997 | 43.5% |
| €51,997 – €81,199 | 45% |
| Above €81,199 | 48% |

### Foreign Income Under IFICI

Unlike the old NHR, the IFICI regime does **not automatically exempt foreign income**. Foreign income is generally taxed at standard Portuguese rates unless it qualifies under a tax treaty.

## Legacy NHR (Still Applicable for Grandfathered Residents)

If you were granted NHR **before January 2024**, your benefits are protected for your original 10-year period:

| Income Type | Legacy NHR Rate |
|---|---|
| Employment income (qualifying) | 20% flat |
| Foreign pensions | 10% flat |
| Foreign dividends, interest | 0% (exempt) |
| Foreign rental income | 0% (exempt) |
| Capital gains from foreign assets | 0% (exempt) |

## Head-to-Head Comparison

### For Employed Professionals

| Scenario | UK | Portugal (IFICI) |
|---|---|---|
| Salary €80,000 | ~32% effective rate | 20% flat |
| Salary €150,000 | ~42% effective rate | 20% flat |
| Dividend income | 33.75%+ | Standard rates (unless NHR legacy) |

**Winner for employed professionals:** Portugal (IFICI), especially above £50,000.

---

### For Retirees

| Scenario | UK | Portugal (Legacy NHR) |
|---|---|---|
| Foreign pension (€40,000) | Progressive (up to 40%) | 10% flat |
| UK state pension | Taxable income | Taxed under treaty (typically 10%) |
| Investment income | 20–39.35% (dividends) | 0% (legacy NHR) |

**Winner for retirees:** Portugal with legacy NHR overwhelmingly. New IFICI applicants need to assess carefully.

---

### For Remote Workers and Freelancers

| Scenario | UK | Portugal (IFICI) |
|---|---|---|
| Freelance income €60,000 | ~30–35% effective | 20% flat (if qualifying activity) |
| Multiple clients, global | High complexity | Medium complexity |
| Social contributions | NICs ~10–13% | SS contributions ~11% |

**Winner:** Portugal for qualifying freelancers, but activity must qualify.

### For Capital Gains

| Scenario | UK | Portugal |
|---|---|---|

| Shares gain £50,000 | 10–20% | 28% standard |

**Winner:** UK generally for capital gains (especially on shares), unless you hold legacy NHR.

## Key Practical Considerations

### Portugal Pros
- 20% flat tax on qualifying income for 10 years
- Lower cost of living (outside Lisbon)
- Warm climate and quality of life
- EU residency, Schengen travel
- Legacy NHR still very attractive for grandfathered residents

### Portugal Cons
- IFICI qualification requires specific job categories
- Foreign income no longer automatically exempt under new regime
- Bureaucracy can be slow
- Lisbon/Porto property prices rising significantly

### UK Pros
- No relocation required
- No need to qualify for a special regime
- Capital gains rates lower for shares
- Strong legal framework, familiar language

### UK Cons
- Progressive tax up to 45%
- High cost of living (especially London)
- No territorial income tax — worldwide income taxed
- Dividend tax rates are high

---

## Who Should Consider Portugal?

- **Qualified professionals** earning above £60,000/year in tech, science, R&D
- **Legacy NHR holders** — continue benefiting until your 10-year period ends
- **Retirees** with existing NHR (10% pension tax is extremely competitive)
- **Remote workers** for non-UK companies willing to relocate

## Who Should Stay in the UK (for Tax Purposes)?

- People with significant capital gains expected on UK shares or property
- Those earning below £50,000 (UK rates are competitive at lower income levels)
- People with complex UK pension arrangements
- Those who don't qualify for IFICI activities
