## Overview

As a digital nomad, you might spend weeks in Bali, months in Lisbon, and winters in Medellín — all while working remotely for clients or employers worldwide. The question almost every nomad asks at some point:

**"Do I have to pay tax, and if so, where?"**

The short answer: **yes, you likely owe tax somewhere** — and in some cases, potentially in two places. This guide explains how to figure out where, and how to legally minimise your burden.

---

## The Core Concept: Tax Residency

Your tax obligations are almost entirely determined by **tax residency**, not citizenship or passport.

Tax residency is typically established by:

1. **The 183-day rule** — spending more than 183 days in a country in a tax year
2. **Domicile / permanent home** — where you have a home available to you
3. **Centre of vital interests** — family, economic ties, social connections
4. **Registration** — formally registering as a resident

### The 183-Day Rule in Practice

| Scenario | Likely Tax Resident? |
|---|---|
| 200 days in Portugal | ✅ Yes — Portuguese tax resident |
| 100 days in each of 4 countries | ❓ Depends on each country's rules |
| No more than 182 days anywhere | ⚠️ Could still trigger residency via other criteria |

> **Warning:** The 183-day rule is a common threshold, but not universal. Germany, UK, and Australia have additional tests that can trigger residency even below 183 days.

---

## Scenario: Can You Be Tax Resident Nowhere?

In theory, yes — it's called **stateless tax residency**. In practice, it is:

- **Risky** — your home country may continue to treat you as resident
- **Difficult to prove** — requires clean break from prior country
- **Subject to scrutiny** — tax authorities actively pursue nomads

Countries may assert you are still a resident if you:
- Maintain a home there
- Have family living there
- Keep bank accounts or investments there
- Return regularly

## Country of Origin Rules

### If You're From the US

Americans are taxed on **worldwide income regardless of where they live**. You must file a US tax return every year, even if you're abroad full-time.

Mitigations available:
- **Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (FEIE)** — exclude up to ~$126,500/year if you qualify
- **Foreign Tax Credit (FTC)** — offset US taxes with taxes paid abroad

### If You're From the EU / UK / Australia / Canada

Most countries (unlike the US) tax based on **residency**. If you genuinely break residency by:
- Leaving for more than 183 days
- De-registering and giving up your home
- Cutting ties (bank accounts, driving licence, etc.)

...you may legally owe **nothing** in your home country.

## The Double Taxation Trap

Double taxation occurs when **two countries each claim the right to tax the same income**.

This can happen if:
- You spend 183+ days in Country A but didn't properly terminate residency in Country B
- Your home country taxes worldwide income (e.g., the US)
- You earn income in a country but don't reside there

### How Tax Treaties Help

Most countries have bilateral **Double Tax Agreements (DTAs)**. These treaties:

- Designate which country has primary taxing rights on each income type
- Provide tie-breaker rules if you qualify as resident in both
- Allow tax credits so the same income isn't taxed twice at full rates

**Check:** Does your home country have a tax treaty with the country you're staying in?

## Practical Checklist for Digital Nomads

### Before You Leave

- [ ] Consult a tax advisor in your home country about exit procedures
- [ ] Understand your home country's residency-breaking requirements
- [ ] Research tax treaties between your home country and planned destinations

### While Travelling

- [ ] Track days spent in each country (use apps like Nomad Tax, Taxbird, or a simple spreadsheet)
- [ ] Keep records of your income sources and amounts
- [ ] Avoid triggering permanent establishment if working for a single employer

### Choosing Where to Establish Residency

- [ ] Look for countries with territorial taxation (only tax local income)
- [ ] Consider digital nomad visa programmes for legal residency
- [ ] Evaluate tax treaties with your income sources' countries

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Assuming leaving your home country ends tax liability | Back taxes, penalties |
| Spending 183+ days in a high-tax country accidentally | Unexpected residency and tax bill |
| Not keeping day-count records | Unable to prove non-residency if challenged |
| Ignoring home country exit requirements | Continued worldwide tax obligations |
| Not reporting foreign bank accounts (US citizens) | FBAR/FATCA fines |
