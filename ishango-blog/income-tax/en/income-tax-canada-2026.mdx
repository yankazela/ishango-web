## Overview

Canada uses a **progressive income tax system** made up of:

- **Federal tax**
- **Provincial or territorial tax**

Both are applied separately, then combined to determine your total tax payable.

Tax rates range from **15% to 33% federally**, with additional provincial rates depending on where you live.

Your final tax depends on:
- Total annual income
- Province or territory of residence
- Tax credits and deductions
- Employment vs self-employment status

---

## Federal Tax Brackets (2026)

| Taxable Income | Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to $55,867 | 15% |
| $55,868 – $111,733 | 20.5% |
| $111,734 – $173,205 | 26% |
| $173,206 – $246,752 | 29% |
| Above $246,753 | 33% |

## Provincial Tax (Example: Ontario)

Each province has its own tax brackets.

### Ontario Tax Brackets (2026)

| Taxable Income | Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to $51,446 | 5.05% |
| $51,447 – $102,894 | 9.15% |
| $102,895 – $150,000 | 11.16% |
| $150,001 – $220,000 | 12.16% |
| Above $220,001 | 13.16% |

> Your total tax = **Federal tax + Provincial tax**

## How Income Tax is Calculated

### Step-by-Step:

1. Start with **gross annual income**
2. Subtract deductions:
   - RRSP contributions
   - Employment expenses
3. Get **taxable income**
4. Apply federal tax brackets
5. Apply provincial tax brackets
6. Subtract tax credits
7. Add additional contributions (CPP, EI)

---

## Example Calculation (Ontario)

Income: **$80,000/year**

### Federal Tax:
- First $55,867 → 15%
- Remaining $24,133 → 20.5%
- Federal tax ≈ **$12,000**

### Provincial Tax:
- Blended rates → ≈ **$4,500**

### Total Before Credits:
- ≈ **$16,500**

### After Basic Credits:
- Final tax ≈ **$13,500 – $14,500**

Effective tax rate ≈ **17–18%**

## Key Tax Credits

Canada uses **tax credits** to reduce your tax bill.

### Federal Credits

- Basic Personal Amount (~$15,705)
- Canada Employment Amount
- Tuition credits
- Medical expenses

### Provincial Credits

- Vary by province
- Ontario includes:
  - Basic personal amount
  - Ontario Trillium Benefit (separate payments)

---

## CPP and EI Contributions

In addition to income tax, employees pay:

### Canada Pension Plan (CPP)
- ~5.95% of income (up to yearly maximum)

### Employment Insurance (EI)
- ~1.66% of income (capped)

These are **not taxes**, but mandatory contributions.

---

## Effective vs Marginal Tax Rate

- **Marginal rate**: Tax on your next dollar earned
- **Effective rate**: Average tax paid overall

Example:
- Income: $80,000
- Marginal rate: ~29–30% (combined)
- Effective rate: ~17–18%

---

## Filing Your Tax Return

Taxes are filed annually with the **Canada Revenue Agency (CRA)**.

### Deadline:
- April 30 (most individuals)
- June 15 (self-employed, payment still due April 30)

### Methods:
- NETFILE (online)
- Tax software
- Accountant

---

## Tax Optimization Tips

- Contribute to an RRSP to reduce taxable income
- Use a TFSA for tax-free growth
- Claim all eligible credits
- Split income (if applicable for families)
- Track deductible expenses carefully

---

## Common Mistakes

- Ignoring provincial taxes
- Not claiming available credits
- Confusing marginal and effective tax rates
- Missing RRSP contribution opportunities
